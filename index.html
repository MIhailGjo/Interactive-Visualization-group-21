Write-up

Dataset and question

We use the Daily Weather in the U.S., 2017 dataset, which contains daily measurements from NOAA's Global Historical Climatology Network (GHCN-Daily) for U.S. weather stations, with sparse stations filtered out. Our question is: which stations recorded the most extreme weather in 2017? We focus on three variables: daily maximum temperature (TMAX, °F), daily precipitation (PRCP, inches) and fastest 5-second wind gust (WSF5, mph). For each station we keep its single highest reading in the selected period and show the top 10.

Implemented interactions

  • Dynamic queries (Category I): the weather category and month dropdowns re-query the data and re-rank the stations immediately.
  • Day range slider (Category I): after picking a month, a two-handle slider sets the lower and upper day, and the ranking uses only the days in between. Bars animate (d3.easeLinear) to their new length and rank, so you can watch the leaders change over the month.
  • State filter (Category I): a state dropdown limits the ranking to stations in one state, so you can find each state's local champions. It combines with the category, month and day filters.
  • Extreme / single-day toggle: switches the ranking between the highest extreme in the day range, and the readings from only one day (the slider then has a single handle).
  • Details-on-demand (Category II): hovering a bar shows a tooltip with the station name, state, exact value and the date of the reading.
  • Highlighting (Category II): the hovered bar is outlined and faded so it stands out from the others.

Design rationale

A "top 10" ranking is a comparison of one quantity across categories, so we used a horizontal bar chart: bar length on a common zero baseline is the most accurately read encoding for magnitude, and horizontal bars leave room for long station names. Bars are sorted from highest to lowest so the winner is always on top, and each bar carries its value as a direct label so exact numbers can be read without the axis. Each category has its own hue (red for heat, blue for rain, teal for wind) so the active category is recognizable at a glance; color is not used to encode data values.

We chose dropdown filters because the questions users ask are discrete ("which station was hottest in July?"), and a dropdown keeps the choice and its current value visible. Tooltips keep the chart uncluttered while still giving the date and state on demand. We considered a map of stations, but a map mostly encodes location, while our question is about ranking magnitudes, so a map would make the comparison harder. We also considered ranking by state, but state-level aggregates hide the single extreme readings we wanted to show.

Data cleaning: missing readings are skipped and simple plausibility limits are applied (TMAX between −80 and 140 °F, PRCP between 0 and 40 in, WSF5 between 0 and 150 mph). These are our own limits, not official NOAA quality flags. The dataset covers January 1 to September 21, 2017.
