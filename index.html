Write-up

Dataset and Research Question

We used the Daily Weather in the U.S., 2017 dataset from class, which is based on NOAA's GHCN-Daily records from weather stations across the United States. We wanted to find out which stations recorded the most extreme weather, so we focused on three measurements: maximum temperature (TMAX), precipitation (PRCP), and the fastest five-second wind gust (WSF5). For each category, we took every station's highest valid reported reading within the selected period and ranked the top 10 stations.

Interactive Features

Our main visualization is a horizontal bar chart that works as a leaderboard. Users can switch between Hottest, Rainiest, and Windiest while filtering the rankings by month or state. We also included a day slider that lets users select a range of days or switch to single-day mode to look at one specific date. The bars animate as the rankings change, making it easier to see which stations move up or down.

Clicking a bar opens Station Spotlight, where the selected station turns gold in the leaderboard and a second chart shows its daily measurements. The chart keeps the surrounding dates visible, with selected days highlighted in gold and the others faded for comparison. Hovering over bars in either chart shows the exact value and date. We also added warnings to certain unusually high readings that haven't been independently verified.

Design Decisions and Alternatives

We chose a horizontal bar chart because our main goal was comparing weather measurements across stations, and bar lengths make those differences easier to see. Horizontal bars also give us enough room for longer station names, while sorting from highest to lowest makes the rankings easier to follow. We used different colors for temperature, rainfall, and wind, with gold highlighting for the selected station and dates.

Dropdowns made sense for choosing a category, month, or state, while a slider worked better for exploring specific days and date ranges. We also considered using a map, but since our question focused on comparing recorded measurements rather than station locations, we decided a leaderboard would be more useful. Station Spotlight was added because a station's highest reading alone doesn't show what happened on the surrounding days.

Findings and Data Limitations

While exploring the data, we noticed some unusually high readings, including a reported temperature of 129.92°F at Patuxent River NAS in Maryland. However, Port Arthur SE TX AP also recorded 26.03 inches of rainfall during Hurricane Harvey, showing that an extreme measurement isn't necessarily incorrect. Rather than removing questionable readings without enough evidence, we kept them and added warnings where further verification was needed. We also changed Station Spotlight to leave gaps for missing daily observations. Since the dataset only covers January 1 through September 21, 2017, our rankings don't represent the entire year.
